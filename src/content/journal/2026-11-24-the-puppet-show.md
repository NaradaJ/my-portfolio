You know puppets, right? Simple movable figures of a person or an animal, controlled from above with strings. Some are worked by hand, but let's stick with the string kind, because we do love to worship things above us.

Every string is tied to a specific part of the puppet. Pull it and that part follows. But move the string to a different attachment point and the puppet won't react the same way, because each part is built differently.

So who pulls the strings? I don't know if there's a divine being up there. What I do know is that the strings of billions of puppets are tangled together. One small movement sets off millions of anonymous reactions. That's the butterfly effect. And Sir Isaac Newton, the most hated person among science students, had it too: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

What if we're all puppets in one huge show? It can feel like free will has flown out the window. So is there anything we can do? Picture the puppet, its strings, and that tangled ball above it. What are our options?

I've found two.

## 1. Move the string

Reattach the string somewhere else, based on the result you want.

For example, I get angry when my girlfriend blames me again and again without telling me why. I don't want to get angry with her. So I delete the long messages without reading them, or on a call I move the phone away for a moment and just say "hm." I'm still working out what I'll do after we're married. Maybe think about something else while I listen.

## 2. Change the puppet

Change how you're built. Going to the gym does that. So does what you eat, the medicine you take, even your coffee. At work, it's learning new skills and sharpening them. Two years ago, code looked like Egyptian hieroglyphs to me. Now I can read it.

## The cheat code

Use the first option to power the second. Once you can see which strings move you, you can start changing how you're built.

We may not be able to change the whole puppet show. But we can put on our own, and that might spark a change across the entire show.
